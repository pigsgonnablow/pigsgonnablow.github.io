    What's happening under the hood

    1 · Sample

    An SDR digitises a whole slice of spectrum at once. Each row of the terrain is one FFT of that slice: height and colour are signal strength, and older rows scroll away from you.

    2 · Demodulate

    Tuning picks a narrow window out of that slice and turns it into sound: AM for broadcasts, SSB for voice, CW for Morse. Digital modes like FT8 and RTTY sound like chirps and warbles.

    3 · Listen

    The audio is decoded into PCM samples and scheduled through the Web Audio API, which runs in any modern browser. three.js draws the terrain with one shader on the GPU.

    Demo band is synthesised in your browser: a Morse beacon, FT8, RTTY, an AM broadcast, a voice QSO and a drifting carrier on a pretend 40 m band. Live KiwiSDR puts every public receiver on the globe, using a list your server refreshes hourly from the KiwiSDR directory. Picking one checks it still has a free slot, then streams its audio and waterfall through your server's relay; if it's full, the next best receiver is tried automatically. These receivers are run by volunteers with only a few listener slots each, so the page names itself to the receiver and disconnects after 30 idle minutes.
