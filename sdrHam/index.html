      What's happening under the hood

      1 · Sample

      An SDR digitises a whole slice of spectrum at once. Each row of the terrain is one FFT of that slice: height and colour are signal strength, and older rows scroll away from you.

      2 · Demodulate

      Tuning picks a narrow window out of that slice and turns it into sound: AM for broadcasts, SSB for voice, CW for Morse. Digital modes like FT8 and RTTY sound like chirps and warbles.

      3 · Listen

      The audio is decoded into PCM samples and scheduled through the Web Audio API, which runs in any modern browser. three.js draws the terrain with one shader on the GPU.

      Everything you hear comes live from a real receiver. The globe shows every public KiwiSDR, from a list your server refreshes hourly from the KiwiSDR directory. Picking one checks it still has a free slot, then streams its audio and waterfall, through your server's relay when the browser can't connect directly; if it's full, the nearest free receiver is tried instead. These receivers are run by volunteers with only a few listener slots each, so the page names itself to the receiver and disconnects after 30 idle minutes.
